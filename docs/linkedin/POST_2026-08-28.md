## Post

Yesterday I wrote about the ordering tool learning its own safety buffer from what the
kitchen actually used, instead of the number I guessed once and never touched again.

Same idea, one step earlier in the day. Today: the screen I open before anything else.

Here is the kind of thing it exists for. A bill paid in cash, with a heavy discount on
it. One of those is nothing. Three in one night, at one branch, is a question. Nobody
was ever going to spot that by scrolling a POS report at 9am, and I certainly wasn't
going to find it in a spreadsheet at midnight. It sat in the data for months and no
human was ever going to meet it.

So the screen goes looking, every morning, and puts what it found at the top.

Before this, my morning was four POS exports, one spreadsheet, and about an hour before
I knew whether anything had gone wrong the night before. By then the day had already
started making its own decisions.

Now it is one page answering two questions.

What happened yesterday. One sentence with a traffic light: what we sold, how that
compares to the same day last week, where the month lands at this pace. Then three tiles
— month pace, branches that need a look, food cost percentage.

What needs me today. A short list, sorted by how much it costs to ignore. Cash bills
with heavy discounts. A branch running 13% under its own normal. Food cost creeping past
38%. Discounted bills that got reprinted. Every line has a button that opens the page
where I can check it.

Two rules underneath all of it.

Compare a branch to itself, not to the others. Our smallest branch will never out-sell
the biggest, and it shouldn't have to. Down 13% against its own normal week is the
signal worth waking up to.

Count dishes per 100 guests, not per day. If 200 more people walk in, everything sells
more and I have learned nothing. Per head tells me taste changed, and taste is what
moves food cost.

And the rule I keep coming back to: if a number on this page doesn't end in something I
can do, it is decoration. It gets cut. That is why the screen is short.

Tomorrow, the part underneath it — where the numbers come from every night, and what
happens when the sync breaks at 2am.
