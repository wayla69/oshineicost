Yesterday I wrote about the ordering tool learning its own safety buffer from what
the kitchen actually used, instead of the number I guessed once and never touched again.

Today: the screen I open before anything else. We call it the daily brief.

Before this, my morning was four POS exports, one spreadsheet, and about an hour
before I knew whether anything had gone wrong the night before. By then the day had
already started making its own decisions.

Now it is one page, and it answers two questions.

What happened yesterday. One sentence at the top with a traffic light: what we sold,
how that compares to the same day last week, where the month lands if we keep this pace.
Then three tiles - month pace, branches that need a look, food cost percentage.

What needs me today. A short list, sorted by how much it hurts. Three cash bills with
heavy discounts. One branch 13% under its own normal. Food cost creeping past 38%.
Two discounted bills reprinted. Every line has a button that opens the page where I can
actually check it.

Two things I would build the same way again.

Compare a branch to itself, not to the others. Pluak Daeng will never out-sell Ubon,
and it shouldn't have to. Down 13% against its own normal week is the signal.

Count dishes per 100 guests, not per day. If 200 more people walk in, everything sells
more and I have learned nothing. Per head tells me taste changed, and that is what
moves food cost.

The rule I keep coming back to: if a number on this page doesn't end in something I can
do, it is decoration. It gets cut.

Tomorrow, the part underneath it - where the numbers come from every night, and what
happens when the sync breaks at 2am.
